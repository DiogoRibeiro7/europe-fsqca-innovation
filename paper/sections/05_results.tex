\section{Results}

\subsection{Analytical sample}

Report the establishments and weight mass surviving each inclusion rule for the
primary sample and for the management extension, and state explicitly which
population each subsequent result describes. Report the weight concentration and
effective sample size, and the fieldwork year and implied innovation reference
window by country.

\subsection{Pan-European analysis}

Populate this subsection only from generated outputs under
\texttt{results/main/europe/}. The required artifacts are the necessity table,
truth table, contradictory-row diagnostics, diversity diagnostics, and solution
terms for the outcome and its negation. Report the conservative, parsimonious
and intermediate solutions, and classify literals as core or peripheral from the
intermediate solution.

\subsection{Estimand comparison}

Report the pan-European solution under the unweighted, firm-population and
equal-country estimands. Where they disagree, report the disagreement rather
than selecting the most convenient one.

\subsection{Regional configurations}

Compare Northern/Western, Southern, and Central/Eastern solutions using the same
Europe-wide calibration anchors. Do not alter regional membership after viewing
solution patterns. Compare regional and pooled solutions term by term, reporting
shared, region-specific and absent configurations rather than solution strings.

\subsection{Directional portability}

For each source-region term, report availability, consistency, coverage and
bootstrap uncertainty in every target region, in both directions. Report the
share of source terms that transfer with the term count it rests on, and report
how often each term survives resampling of its own source region.

\subsection{Conjunctural dependence and substitution}

Report the pairwise sufficiency tests and the substitutions visible in the
solution structure. Report condition co-occurrence separately and describe it as
co-occurrence. Do not describe any of these as economic complementarity.

\subsection{Causal asymmetry}

Compare high-innovation and low-innovation solution structures without assuming
that one is the logical inverse of the other.
